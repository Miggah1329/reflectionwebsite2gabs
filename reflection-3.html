Reflection 03 — Risk Assessment

Risk Assessment

Scoring risks by likelihood and impact showed me that good risk assessment is about evidence and preparation, not guesswork.

TOPIC: Risk Management Process TOPIC: Risk Assessment

What We Discussed

In our lesson on the Risk Management Process, we learned how organizations identify their important assets, look at the threats and vulnerabilities around them, and decide how serious each risk is based on its likelihood and impact. We applied this in our Barangay Manicahan audit. We listed eight information assets, such as payment and official-receipt records, issued certificates, external-drive backups, and computer passwords. Then we rated six risks on a 1 to 5 scale for likelihood and impact, and multiplied the two to get a score. The highest-rated risks were unauthorized disclosure of information, loss of digital records, and password compromise, which all landed in the High range.

What It Changed

What stood out to me is that a risk is not the same as an incident. The interviewee reported no cyberattack or data loss in about three years of service, but that did not mean the risks were gone. We also learned to keep threats, vulnerabilities, and risks separate, and to mark a weakness as “not verified” instead of assuming a control was missing. Every risk ended with a treatment decision, and for ours we chose to reduce them through controls such as scheduled and tested backups and a written access and confidentiality procedure. We also noted that the ratings should be revisited when new evidence comes in. This taught me that risk assessment is continuous, and that it helps organizations protect their assets and make better decisions about security threats.
