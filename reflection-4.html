Reflection 04 — Information Security Audit

Information Security Audit

Hearing real practices straight from the interviewee, and learning that a reported control is not the same as a tested one.

SOURCE: Barangay Manicahan Interview SOURCE: Information Security Audit

The Interview

For our project, we interviewed a representative of Barangay Manicahan in Zamboanga City to learn how the office protects the records it uses for collecting payments and issuing certificates and clearances. I learned that the Treasurer, Secretary, and staff each have their own password-protected computers, that printed records are kept in designated cabinets, and that the staff and Secretary use external drives for backups. The interviewee also said that the Treasurer and Secretary attended DILG seminars before taking their oath of office. Hearing these practices directly from the interviewee helped me understand how information security works in an actual office, where digital files and paper records both need protection.

What It Changed

This experience made me realize that having security measures is not the same as knowing they work. The interview did not tell us how often backups are made or whether they can be restored, how strong the passwords are, whether the cabinets are locked, or whether there is a written incident response procedure. The interviewee also named staff disclosing information outside the office as a possible weakness. Because we only had an interview, our report had to say “reported” and “not verified” instead of assuming that a control was missing or that everything was fine. It taught me the importance of asking the right questions and carefully evaluating the answers. Overall, it helped me appreciate how important information security is in an organization that handles constituents’ personal and payment information.

Reported

What the interviewee told us

• Own password-protected computers for the Treasurer, Secretary, and staff
• Printed records kept in designated cabinets
• External drives used for backups
• DILG seminars attended before taking oath of office
Not verified

What an interview alone couldn't show

• How often backups are made, and whether they can be restored
• How strong the passwords are
• Whether the cabinets are locked
• Whether there is a written incident response procedure
